### 16.8 Decision-Making in Growth and Exit

#### The Sunk Cost Dilemma

One of the most challenging decisions for entrepreneurs is determining when to continue investing in a struggling venture versus when to terminate it. This decision is complicated by the sunk cost dilemma, where entrepreneurs may continue investing in failing ventures because of the resources already invested.

##### Understanding Sunk Costs

Sunk costs are costs that have already been incurred and cannot be recovered. Rational decision-making should ignore sunk costs when making future investment decisions, but entrepreneurs often struggle with this concept.

**Characteristics of Sunk Costs:**
- **Irrecoverable**: Cannot be recovered regardless of future decisions
- **Past-Focused**: Represent past investments and decisions
- **Irrelevant**: Should not influence future investment decisions
- **Emotional**: Often create emotional attachment to failing ventures
- **Investment-Based**: Result from previous investment decisions
- **Opportunity Cost**: Continuing may prevent investment in better opportunities

- **Resource Availability**: Availability of additional resources
- **Turnaround Potential**: Likelihood of successful turnaround
- **Alternative Uses**: Better uses for available resources

##### Financial Factors

- **Opportunity Cost**: Cost of not investing elsewhere

- **Focus on Future Value**: Evaluate based on future potential
- **Ignore Sunk Costs**: Do not consider unrecoverable investments
- **Assess Realistically**: Evaluate realistically without emotional bias
- **Consider Alternatives**: Evaluate alternative uses of resources
- **Seek External Input**: Get objective external perspectives

##### Evaluation Criteria
- **Market Validation**: Evidence of market demand
- **Product-Market Fit**: Evidence of product-market fit
- **Unit Economics**: Positive unit economics potential
- **Growth Trajectory**: Evidence of improving metrics
- **Competitive Position**: Sustainable competitive advantages
- **Resource Requirements**: Reasonable additional resource needs

### 16.10 Summary

Managing growth and planning for exit are critical aspects of entrepreneurial success. Entrepreneurs must make difficult decisions about when to continue investing in ventures and when to exit. They must also navigate the challenges of scaling operations while maintaining innovation and effectiveness.

Key takeaways include:
- Sunk cost dilemmas require objective decision-making
- Exit planning should begin early in the venture lifecycle
- Succession planning is critical for long-term success
- Growth brings operational and organizational challenges
- Innovation must be maintained during growth
- Professional management skills become increasingly important

Successful entrepreneurs balance growth opportunities with realistic assessment of venture viability. They plan for exits while focusing on creating value during the growth phase. They also prepare their organizations to handle the challenges of scaling while maintaining the entrepreneurial culture that drove initial success.
