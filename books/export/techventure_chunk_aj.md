## Chapter 9: Market Segmentation, Targeting, and Positioning

### Customer Segmentation Strategies

#### Understanding Market Segmentation

Market segmentation is the process of dividing a market into distinct groups of buyers who might require separate products or marketing mixes. For technology ventures, effective segmentation is critical for resource allocation and competitive positioning.

##### Purpose of Market Segmentation
- **Resource Optimization**: Focus limited resources on most promising segments
- **Customer Understanding**: Develop deep understanding of specific needs
- **Product Development**: Tailor products to specific customer requirements
- **Marketing Efficiency**: Create targeted marketing messages
- **Competitive Advantage**: Establish unique positioning in chosen segments
- **Growth Planning**: Identify expansion opportunities

##### Segmentation Criteria

###### Demographic Segmentation
Demographic variables include age, gender, income, education, occupation, and family size.

**Applications for Technology Ventures:**
- **Age Groups**: Different technology adoption patterns
- **Income Levels**: Affordability and purchasing power
- **Education**: Technology literacy and sophistication
- **Occupation**: Professional needs and requirements
- **Family Size**: Household technology needs
- **Life Stage**: Changing technology requirements over time

###### Psychographic Segmentation
Psychographic segmentation divides markets based on lifestyle, personality traits, and values.

**Applications for Technology Ventures:**
- **Lifestyle**: Technology usage patterns
- **Personality**: Technology adoption preferences
- **Values**: Technology importance and priorities
- **Attitudes**: Technology openness and acceptance
- **Interests**: Technology engagement levels
- **Opinions**: Technology perceptions and beliefs

###### Behavioral Segmentation
Behavioral segmentation divides markets based on consumer behavior and usage patterns.

**Applications for Technology Ventures:**
- **Usage Rate**: Heavy, medium, and light users
- **Loyalty Status**: Brand loyalty levels
- **Benefit Sought**: Different benefits valued
- **Purchase Occasion**: Timing of purchases
- **User Status**: New, regular, or former users
- **Readiness Stage**: Awareness, knowledge, or adoption levels

###### Geographic Segmentation
Geographic segmentation divides markets based on location and regional characteristics.

**Applications for Technology Ventures:**
- **Region**: Cultural and economic differences
- **Climate**: Environmental technology needs
- **Population Density**: Urban vs. rural requirements
- **Market Size**: Local market potential
- **Cultural Factors**: Local preferences and customs
- **Infrastructure**: Technology availability and access

#### Target Market Selection

##### Market Attractiveness Factors
When selecting target markets, technology ventures should consider:

**Market Size and Growth**
- Current market size
- Growth rate projections
- Market maturity level
- Expansion opportunities
- Seasonal variations
- Cyclical patterns

**Competition Level**
- Number of competitors
- Competitor strength
- Market share distribution
- Competitive advantages
- Barriers to entry
- Competitive intensity

**Customer Characteristics**
- Customer needs and problems
- Customer purchasing power
- Customer loyalty potential
- Customer accessibility
- Customer service requirements
- Customer retention potential

**Resource Requirements**
- Capital investment needed
- Human resource requirements
- Technology requirements
- Infrastructure needs
- Distribution requirements
- Support requirements

##### Targeting Strategies

###### Undifferentiated Marketing
- Treat the entire market as one segment
- Use single marketing mix for all customers
- Focus on common customer needs
- Economies of scale in production
- Cost efficiencies in marketing
- Risk of missing customer differences

###### Differentiated Marketing
- Target multiple market segments
- Develop separate marketing mixes
- Maximize market coverage
- Increase sales potential
- Higher costs and complexity
- Better customer satisfaction

###### Concentrated Marketing
- Focus on single market segment
- Develop specialized marketing mix
- Achieve deep market knowledge
- Build strong market position
- Higher risk of market changes
- Limited market size

###### Micromarketing
- Target individual customers or small groups
- Customize products and marketing
- Maximize customer satisfaction
- Premium pricing potential
- High costs and complexity
- Requires advanced technology

#### Case Study: InVision Technologies

InVision Technologies provides an excellent example of successful market segmentation and targeting. The company developed computed tomography technology to detect explosives for airport security.

##### Market Analysis
- **Target Segment**: U.S. airport baggage screening
- **Market Size**: Initially slow-growing market
- **Growth Catalyst**: September 11, 2001 terrorist attacks
- **Sales Performance**: 250 machines sold in previous decade, 750 machines in two years following attacks
- **International Expansion**: Planned expansion to global markets
- **Acquisition**: Acquired by GE in 2004 for $900 million

##### Segmentation Success Factors
- **Clear Target Definition**: Focused on airport security market
- **Market Understanding**: Deep knowledge of customer needs
- **Timing**: Capitalized on post-9/11 security needs
- **Technology Fit**: Appropriate technology for market needs
- **Scalability**: Ability to expand with market growth
- **Exit Strategy**: Successful acquisition by major corporation

#### Case Study: Samsung Galaxy

Samsung's Galaxy phone targeting strategy demonstrates effective customer segmentation.

##### Target Segment
- **Primary Segment**: Young adults craving full-featured wireless devices
- **Technology Focus**: Cutting-edge technology preferences
- **Feature Requirements**: Advanced functionality needs
- **Price Sensitivity**: Value-conscious but technology-demanding
- **Brand Awareness**: Tech-savvy and brand-conscious
- **Usage Patterns**: Heavy mobile technology users

##### Segmentation Benefits
- **Focused Marketing**: Targeted marketing messages
- **Product Development**: Features aligned with customer needs
- **Competitive Positioning**: Clear differentiation from competitors
- **Resource Allocation**: Efficient use of marketing resources
- **Customer Satisfaction**: Products meeting specific needs
- **Market Share**: Strong position in target segment

#### Common Segmentation Mistakes

##### Over-Segmentation
Trying to target too many segments simultaneously.

**Solutions:**
- Focus on most attractive segments first
- Prioritize segments based on potential
- Allocate resources strategically
- Build expertise in chosen segments
- Expand gradually after success
- Maintain focus on core segments

##### Under-Segmentation
Not segmenting the market sufficiently.

**Solutions:**
- Conduct thorough market research
- Identify distinct customer needs
- Develop targeted approaches
- Customize marketing messages
- Tailor products to segments
- Measure segment performance

##### Poor Segment Selection
Choosing segments that are not viable.

**Solutions:**
- Evaluate segment attractiveness
- Assess resource requirements
- Consider competitive dynamics
- Validate market demand
- Test assumptions with customers
- Monitor segment performance

##### Inadequate Segment Understanding
Not understanding segment needs deeply.

**Solutions:**
- Conduct customer research
- Engage with segment members
- Test assumptions regularly
- Gather feedback continuously
- Monitor segment changes
- Adapt to evolving needs

#### Target Market Validation

##### Market Research Methods
- **Surveys**: Quantitative customer data
- **Interviews**: Qualitative customer insights
- **Focus Groups**: Group customer feedback
- **Observation**: Customer behavior analysis
- **Analytics**: Customer data analysis
- **Testing**: Market validation experiments

##### Validation Criteria
- **Size**: Sufficient market size
- **Growth**: Growth potential
- **Accessibility**: Reachability of customers
- **Profitability**: Potential for profitability
- **Competition**: Manageable competitive environment
- **Fit**: Alignment with company capabilities

#### Positioning Strategy

##### Positioning Definition
Positioning is the process of arranging for a product to occupy a clear, distinctive, and desirable place relative to competing products in the minds of target consumers.

##### Positioning Process
1. **Identify Competitors**: Determine competitive landscape
2. **Analyze Customer Perceptions**: Understand current positioning
3. **Select Positioning Strategy**: Choose desired position
4. **Develop Positioning Statement**: Articulate position clearly
5. **Implement Positioning**: Execute positioning strategy
6. **Monitor Positioning**: Track positioning effectiveness

##### Positioning Strategies
- **Attribute Positioning**: Based on product attributes
- **Benefit Positioning**: Based on customer benefits
- **Use/Usage Positioning**: Based on usage occasions
- **User Positioning**: Based on user types
- **Competitor Positioning**: Relative to competitors
- **Product Category Positioning**: Based on category membership

#### Redefining Customer Segments: The Flexcar Example

Flexcar, a car-sharing company, provides an example of redefining customer segments to improve market positioning.

##### Original Target Market
- **Broad Market**: General car-sharing users
- **Wide Appeal**: Attempted to serve all potential users
- **General Messaging**: Generic marketing messages
- **Limited Focus**: Scattered marketing efforts
- **Resource Dilution**: Inefficient resource allocation
- **Weak Positioning**: Unclear market position

##### Refined Target Market
- **Specific Segment**: People who want short-term car access
- **Clear Value**: Convenient, flexible car access
- **Targeted Messaging**: Messages addressing specific needs
- **Focused Efforts**: Concentrated marketing resources
- **Efficient Allocation**: Optimized resource use
- **Strong Positioning**: Clear market position

##### Segmentation Refinement Process
1. **Customer Analysis**: Analyze existing customer base
2. **Need Identification**: Identify specific customer needs
3. **Segment Definition**: Define target customer segments
4. **Value Proposition**: Develop segment-specific value propositions
5. **Marketing Strategy**: Create targeted marketing strategies
6. **Performance Monitoring**: Track segment performance

#### Market Segmentation Best Practices

##### Research-Driven Segmentation
Base segmentation on thorough market research rather than assumptions.

**Best Practices:**
- Conduct primary research
- Validate with secondary data
- Test segment assumptions
- Monitor segment changes
- Update segmentation regularly
- Measure segment performance

##### Customer-Centric Approach
Focus on customer needs and behaviors rather than internal company preferences.

**Best Practices:**
- Understand customer problems
- Identify customer benefits
- Map customer journeys
- Analyze customer feedback
- Monitor customer satisfaction
- Adapt to customer changes

##### Actionable Segments
Ensure segments are actionable and profitable.

**Best Practices:**
- Verify segment accessibility
- Assess resource requirements
- Evaluate profitability potential
- Test marketing approaches
- Monitor segment performance
- Adjust as needed

##### Dynamic Segmentation
Recognize that segments evolve over time.

**Best Practices:**
- Monitor market trends
- Track customer behavior changes
- Update segment definitions
- Adapt marketing strategies
- Reassess segment attractiveness
- Plan for segment evolution

### Summary

Market segmentation, targeting, and positioning are fundamental to entrepreneurial success. Technology ventures must carefully analyze markets, select appropriate target segments, and develop clear positioning strategies. The process requires ongoing research, validation, and refinement to ensure continued effectiveness.

Key takeaways include:
- Segmentation enables efficient resource allocation
- Targeting focuses marketing efforts on most promising customers
- Positioning creates clear market differentiation
- Validation ensures segment viability
- Flexibility allows for market evolution
- Customer focus drives segmentation success

By following systematic approaches to market segmentation, technology ventures can improve their chances of success by focusing resources on the most attractive market opportunities and developing offerings that meet specific customer needs.
