# The Startup Owner's Manual (continued)

## Chapter 11: Customer Development Team and Pivoting

### Building the Customer Development Team

The customer development team is responsible for executing the customer development process. It requires specific skills and roles that differ from traditional business execution.

#### Purpose of the Customer Development Team
The customer development team serves to:
- Execute customer discovery and validation
- Test business model hypotheses
- Gather customer feedback
- Iterate business models based on learning
- Pivot when necessary
- Transition to customer creation

#### Team Composition

##### Founder Leadership
The founder must lead customer development activities because:
- Only founders have authority to pivot
- Founders must hear customer feedback directly
- Founders have the vision to see implications
- Founders have the commitment to make changes
- Founders understand the business model
- Founders can make quick decisions

##### Customer Development Skills
The team needs specific skills for customer development:
- Customer interviewing skills
- Hypothesis testing abilities
- Data analysis capabilities
- Iteration and learning mindset
- Pivoting decision-making
- Customer empathy and listening

##### Traditional vs. Customer Development Skills
Traditional managers may not be suitable because:
- They're trained in execution, not discovery
- They focus on repeatable processes
- They're not comfortable with uncertainty
- They may not be creative in searching
- They're not used to frequent pivots
- They prefer planning over learning

#### Team Roles and Responsibilities

##### Customer Discovery Roles
During customer discovery, the team should:
- Conduct customer interviews
- Test problem hypotheses
- Validate solution concepts
- Document learning and insights
- Iterate business model assumptions
- Prepare for validation phase

##### Customer Validation Roles
During customer validation, the team should:
- Test sales processes
- Validate marketing messages
- Confirm business model
- Train sales and marketing teams
- Establish metrics and tracking
- Prepare for customer creation

##### Customer Creation Roles
During customer creation, the team should:
- Execute proven processes
- Scale marketing and sales
- Expand market reach
- Optimize customer acquisition
- Focus on predictable growth
- Build operational infrastructure

##### Company Building Roles
During company building, the team should:
- Build formal processes
- Scale the organization
- Establish departments
- Implement controls
- Prepare for growth or exit
- Transition to traditional management

#### Team Structure

##### Small Team Structure
For early-stage startups, the team might include:
- Founder/CEO leading the process
- One or two customer development specialists
- Technical co-founder for product validation
- One sales/marketing person for validation
- Administrative support for documentation
- External advisors for guidance

##### Medium Team Structure
For growing startups, the team might expand to include:
- Customer development manager
- Multiple customer development reps
- Sales development representatives
- Marketing specialists
- Product managers
- Business development personnel

##### Large Team Structure
For scaling startups, the team might include:
- VP of customer development
- Customer development teams by function
- Sales organization
- Marketing organization
- Product development teams
- Business development teams

### The Pivoting Process

Pivoting is a structured course correction designed to test a new fundamental hypothesis about the product, business model, or engine of growth.

#### Purpose of Pivoting
Pivoting serves to:
- Test new business model hypotheses
- Correct course when assumptions are invalid
- Adapt to market feedback
- Find viable business models
- Avoid business failure
- Preserve company resources

#### Types of Pivots

##### Zoom-in Pivot
Focus on one feature as the whole product.

###### Characteristics
- Take one feature and make it the entire product
- Focus on core value proposition
- Simplify the offering
- Target specific use case
- Reduce complexity
- Focus resources

###### Examples
- PayPal started as a security software company before pivoting to focus on mobile payments and then to online payments
- Instagram started as Burbn, a location-based social network, before pivoting to focus on photo sharing

##### Zoom-out Pivot
Make the whole product one feature of a larger product.

###### Characteristics
- Expand the product scope
- Add complementary features
- Address broader problems
- Increase product complexity
- Target larger market
- Expand value proposition

###### Examples
- Slack started as Tiny Speck, developing an online game, before pivoting to focus on team communication tools
- YouTube started as a dating site before pivoting to video sharing

##### Customer Segment Pivot
Target different customers with the same product.

###### Characteristics
- Keep the product the same
- Change target customer segment
- Address different customer needs
- Enter new market segments
- Leverage existing product
- Adapt go-to-market strategy

###### Examples
- Flickr started as a gaming company before pivoting to focus on photo sharing for a broader audience
- Groupon started as The Point, a social activism platform, before pivoting to group buying

##### Customer Need Pivot
Solve different problems for the same customers.

###### Characteristics
- Keep the same customer segment
- Address different customer problems
- Maintain customer relationships
- Leverage customer understanding
- Expand value proposition
- Address adjacent needs

###### Examples
- Nintendo started making playing cards before pivoting to toys and then to video games
- Starbucks started selling espresso machines before pivoting to coffee shops

##### Platform Pivot
Move from an application to a platform or vice versa.

###### Characteristics
- Change business model fundamentally
- Shift from product to platform
- Or shift from platform to product
- Change value proposition
- Alter go-to-market strategy
- Transform business model

###### Examples
- Salesforce started as a CRM application before pivoting to a platform for other applications
- Magento started as a shopping cart before pivoting to an e-commerce platform

##### Business Model Pivot
Change the business model while keeping the product.

###### Characteristics
- Change how value is captured
- Maintain product offering
- Alter revenue model
- Change pricing strategy
- Modify cost structure
- Adjust value proposition

###### Examples
- Adobe shifted from perpetual software licenses to subscription model
- Netflix shifted from DVD rental to streaming subscription

##### Value Capture Pivot
Change how value is captured while keeping the same value proposition.

###### Characteristics
- Maintain value proposition
- Change revenue model
- Alter pricing approach
- Modify monetization
- Adjust business model
- Preserve customer value

###### Examples
- Spotify offers both free ad-supported and paid subscription models
- Many apps offer freemium models

##### Engine of Growth Pivot
Change the growth mechanism.

###### Characteristics
- Change growth strategy
- Alter acquisition approach
- Modify retention strategy
- Adjust referral mechanisms
- Change growth model
- Transform scaling approach

###### Examples
- LinkedIn shifted from viral growth to professional networking focus
- Facebook shifted from college students to broader audience

##### Channel Pivot
Change how products are delivered to customers.

###### Characteristics
- Maintain product offering
- Change distribution approach
- Alter go-to-market strategy
- Modify customer acquisition
- Adjust sales model
- Transform delivery method

###### Examples
- Many retailers shifted from physical to online channels
- Software companies shifted from direct sales to channel partnerships

##### Technology Pivot
Change the technology while keeping the same value proposition.

###### Characteristics
- Maintain value proposition
- Change underlying technology
- Improve product performance
- Reduce costs
- Enhance capabilities
- Modernize approach

###### Examples
- Companies migrating from on-premise to cloud technology
- Mobile app platforms adapting to new operating systems

#### Pivoting Decision Framework

##### When to Pivot
Pivot when:
- Customer feedback contradicts business model
- Market data invalidates assumptions
- Product-market fit isn't achieved
- Customer acquisition costs are too high
- Market size is smaller than expected
- Competitive threats emerge

##### When to Persevere
Persevere when:
- Market feedback confirms business model
- Customer metrics are improving
- Product-market fit is evident
- Customer acquisition is working
- Market size is adequate
- Competitive position is strong

##### Pivoting Process
1. Gather customer feedback and market data
2. Analyze business model assumptions
3. Identify problematic hypotheses
4. Generate pivot alternatives
5. Evaluate pivot options
6. Select optimal pivot approach
7. Implement pivot quickly
8. Test new business model
9. Measure results
10. Iterate or pivot again if needed

#### Pivoting Best Practices

##### Speed and Agility
- Pivot quickly when necessary
- Minimize pivot costs
- Maintain team morale
- Communicate changes effectively
- Preserve customer relationships
- Learn from pivots

##### Documentation and Learning
- Document pivot decisions
- Track pivot outcomes
- Learn from pivots
- Share pivot knowledge
- Build pivot expertise
- Improve pivot process

##### Team Management
- Maintain team during pivots
- Communicate pivot rationale
- Align team on new direction
- Preserve key personnel
- Manage pivot stress
- Celebrate pivot successes

Pivoting is a critical skill for startup success. Master the ability to pivot quickly and effectively based on customer feedback and market learning.
