### 17.6 Financial Planning and Analysis

#### Break-Even Analysis

Break-even analysis is a fundamental tool for understanding the relationship between costs, volume, and profits. It helps entrepreneurs determine the sales volume required to cover all costs.

##### Break-Even Point Calculation

The break-even point is calculated as:

Break-Even Point (Units) = Fixed Costs ÷ (Price per Unit - Variable Cost per Unit)

**Example Exercise 17.6:**
Sensor International's costs include:
- Variable cost for manufacturing, marketing, and distributing a package of six sensors: 14 cents
- Price to the distributor: 68 cents
- One-time fixed costs: $121,000

**Calculation:**
- Contribution margin per unit = $0.68 - $0.14 = $0.54
- Break-even units = $121,000 ÷ $0.54 = 224,074 units

Therefore, Sensor International needs to sell approximately 224,074 packages to break even.

#### Return on Investment (ROI) Analysis

ROI measures the profitability of an investment relative to its cost.

##### ROI Formula
ROI = (Net Profit ÷ Investment) × 100

**Example Exercise 17.7:**
Continuing with Sensor International:
- Year 1 sales: 300,000 packages
- Year 2 sales: 400,000 packages
- Original investment: $100,000
- Tax rate: 20%

**Year 1 Analysis:**
- Revenue: 300,000 × $0.68 = $204,000
- Variable costs: 300,000 × $0.14 = $42,000
- Contribution margin: $204,000 - $42,000 = $162,000
- Fixed costs: $121,000
- EBIT: $162,000 - $121,000 = $41,000
- Tax: $41,000 × 0.20 = $8,200
- Net income: $41,000 - $8,200 = $32,800
- ROI Year 1: ($32,800 ÷ $100,000) × 100 = 32.8%

**Year 2 Analysis:**
- Revenue: 400,000 × $0.68 = $272,000
- Variable costs: 400,000 × $0.14 = $56,000
- Contribution margin: $272,000 - $56,000 = $216,000
- Fixed costs: $121,000
- EBIT: $216,000 - $121,000 = $95,000
- Tax: $95,000 × 0.20 = $19,000
- Net income: $95,000 - $19,000 = $76,000
- ROI Year 2: ($76,000 ÷ $100,000) × 100 = 76.0%

#### Pricing Strategy and Profitability

When fixed costs change or sales volumes are limited, pricing strategy becomes critical for profitability.

**Example Exercise 17.8:**
Reconsidering the firm with:
- Fixed costs declined to $30,000
- Expected sales: 60 units this year
- Need to determine profitable price

To ensure profitability, the price must cover both variable and fixed costs:
- Required contribution margin per unit = ($30,000 ÷ 60 units) + $0.14
- Required contribution margin per unit = $500 + $0.14 = $500.14
- Minimum profitable price = $500.14 + $0.14 = $500.28

This example illustrates how low sales volumes require significantly higher prices to achieve profitability.

#### Financial Ratios and Performance Metrics

##### Profitability Ratios
- **Gross Profit Margin**: (Revenue - COGS) ÷ Revenue
- **Operating Profit Margin**: Operating Income ÷ Revenue
- **Net Profit Margin**: Net Income ÷ Revenue
- **Return on Assets (ROA)**: Net Income ÷ Total Assets
- **Return on Equity (ROE)**: Net Income ÷ Shareholders' Equity
- **Return on Investment (ROI)**: Net Income ÷ Investment

##### Efficiency Ratios
- **Asset Turnover**: Revenue ÷ Total Assets
- **Inventory Turnover**: COGS ÷ Average Inventory
- **Accounts Receivable Turnover**: Revenue ÷ Average Receivables
- **Working Capital Turnover**: Revenue ÷ Working Capital
- **Fixed Asset Turnover**: Revenue ÷ Net Fixed Assets
- **Total Asset Turnover**: Revenue ÷ Total Assets

##### Liquidity Ratios
- **Current Ratio**: Current Assets ÷ Current Liabilities
- **Quick Ratio**: (Current Assets - Inventory) ÷ Current Liabilities
- **Cash Ratio**: Cash ÷ Current Liabilities
- **Operating Cash Flow Ratio**: Operating Cash Flow ÷ Current Liabilities
- **Days Sales Outstanding**: (Receivables ÷ Revenue) × 365
- **Days Inventory Outstanding**: (Inventory ÷ COGS) × 365

##### Leverage Ratios
- **Debt-to-Equity**: Total Debt ÷ Total Equity
- **Debt-to-Assets**: Total Debt ÷ Total Assets
- **Equity Ratio**: Total Equity ÷ Total Assets
- **Times Interest Earned**: EBIT ÷ Interest Expense
- **Debt Service Coverage**: Operating Income ÷ Total Debt Service
- **Financial Leverage**: Total Assets ÷ Total Equity

#### Cost of Capital

##### Weighted Average Cost of Capital (WACC)
WACC represents the average cost of all capital sources.

**Formula:**
WACC = (E/V × Re) + (D/V × Rd × (1-T))

Where:
- E = Market value of equity
- D = Market value of debt
- V = E + D (total value)
- Re = Cost of equity
- Rd = Cost of debt
- T = Tax rate

##### Cost of Equity
- **CAPM**: Capital Asset Pricing Model
- **Dividend Growth Model**: Based on dividend growth
- **Risk Premium**: Risk premium approach
- **Comparable Companies**: Based on similar companies
- **Venture Capital Method**: For early-stage companies
- **Build-up Method**: Adding risk premiums
