### How Do Successful Entrepreneurs Create a Compelling Business Design for Their New Ventures?

A new business should develop a clear vision or long-term destiny and a mission statement that captures its goals. Any new business is defined by the wants or needs that customers satisfy when they buy a product or service. To create a theory of a new business, the entrepreneur must cogently and clearly describe the customers and their needs and how the new venture will satisfy those needs. A business model describes the relationship between the wants and needs of customers and the products or services that satisfy those wants and needs.

### 3.1 The Vision

The vision represents the long-term destination of the company. It articulates what the company aspires to become in the future and provides direction for the organization.

#### Characteristics of Effective Visions
- **Inspiring**: Motivates and energizes stakeholders
- **Clear**: Easily understood by all stakeholders
- **Focused**: Provides clear direction for the organization
- **Flexible**: Allows for adaptation as circumstances change
- **Desirable**: Creates a compelling picture of the future
- **Feasible**: Realistic and achievable

#### Vision Development Process
1. **Environmental Analysis**: Understand market trends, customer needs, and competitive dynamics
2. **Internal Assessment**: Evaluate company capabilities, resources, and culture
3. **Stakeholder Input**: Gather perspectives from key stakeholders
4. **Future Projection**: Envision where the company should be in 5-10 years
5. **Communication**: Clearly articulate the vision to all stakeholders

#### Vision Examples
- **Google**: "To organize the world's information and make it universally accessible and useful"
- **Tesla**: "To accelerate the world's transition to sustainable energy"
- **Amazon**: "To be Earth's most customer-centric company"

### 3.2 The Mission Statement

The mission statement defines the company's purpose and primary objectives. It explains why the company exists and what it aims to achieve in the short to medium term.

#### Components of Effective Mission Statements
- **Purpose**: Why the company exists
- **Target Customers**: Who the company serves
- **Products/Services**: What the company offers
- **Markets**: Where the company operates
- **Values**: How the company conducts business
- **Technology**: How technology is leveraged

#### Mission Statement Development
1. **Purpose Clarification**: Define the fundamental reason for existence
2. **Customer Focus**: Identify primary customer segments
3. **Value Proposition**: Articulate the value provided
4. **Market Definition**: Specify target markets
5. **Values Integration**: Incorporate core values
6. **Technology Consideration**: Address technology role

#### Mission Statement Examples
- **Nike**: "To bring inspiration and innovation to every athlete in the world"
- **Facebook**: "To give people the power to build community and bring the world closer together"
- **Microsoft**: "To empower every person and every organization on the planet to achieve more"

### 3.3 The Value Proposition

The value proposition articulates the unique value that the company offers to its customers. It explains why customers should choose the company's products or services over competitors.

#### Elements of Value Propositions
- **Customer Segments**: Specific groups of customers served
- **Value Map**: Benefits delivered to each customer segment
- **Pain Relievers**: Solutions to customer problems
- **Gain Creators**: Benefits that exceed customer expectations
- **Products and Services**: Specific offerings that deliver value

#### Value Proposition Canvas
The value proposition canvas aligns customer jobs, pains, and gains with products and services:

**Customer Profile**:
- Customer Jobs: Functional, social, and emotional tasks
- Pains: Negative emotions, costs, and risks
- Gains: Desired outcomes and benefits

**Value Map**:
- Products and Services: Specific offerings
- Pain Relievers: Solutions to customer problems
- Gain Creators: Features that exceed expectations

#### Value Proposition Development Process
1. **Customer Research**: Understand customer needs and preferences
2. **Competitive Analysis**: Identify gaps in competitor offerings
3. **Value Identification**: Determine unique value elements
4. **Testing**: Validate value proposition with customers
5. **Iteration**: Refine based on feedback
6. **Communication**: Clearly articulate to stakeholders

### 3.4 The Business Model

The business model describes how the company creates, delivers, and captures value. It encompasses all the elements necessary for business success.

#### Business Model Components
- **Value Propositions**: Benefits delivered to customers
- **Customer Segments**: Groups of people or organizations served
- **Channels**: How the company communicates with customer segments
- **Customer Relationships**: Types of relationships established
- **Revenue Streams**: Cash generated from each customer segment
- **Key Resources**: Assets required for business model
- **Key Activities**: Most important activities performed
- **Key Partnerships**: Network of suppliers and partners
- **Cost Structure**: Costs incurred to operate the business model

#### Business Model Development
1. **Hypothesis Formation**: Create initial business model hypotheses
2. **Customer Validation**: Test assumptions with real customers
3. **Iteration**: Refine based on learning and feedback
4. **Validation**: Confirm business model viability
5. **Documentation**: Record validated business model
6. **Execution**: Implement the business model

### 3.5 Business Model Innovation in Challenging Markets

In challenging markets, entrepreneurs must innovate their business models to create value and achieve success.

#### Challenges in Difficult Markets
- **Resource Constraints**: Limited access to capital and talent
- **Market Resistance**: Customer reluctance to adopt new solutions
- **Regulatory Barriers**: Complex or restrictive regulations
- **Competition**: Established players with significant advantages
- **Infrastructure**: Inadequate supporting infrastructure
- **Cultural Barriers**: Resistance to change or new approaches

#### Innovation Strategies
- **Resourcefulness**: Creative use of limited resources
- **Local Adaptation**: Customization for local conditions
- **Regulatory Navigation**: Creative compliance approaches
- **Partnership Building**: Strategic alliances with key players
- **Infrastructure Development**: Investment in supporting infrastructure
- **Cultural Sensitivity**: Respect for local customs and practices

### 3.6 Spotlight on Stratasys

Stratasys provides an example of business model innovation in the 3D printing industry. The company developed a unique approach to 3D printing technology that created significant value for customers while building a sustainable competitive advantage.

#### Stratasys Business Model Innovation
- **Technology Leadership**: Pioneering 3D printing technology
- **Market Education**: Building awareness and demand
- **Ecosystem Development**: Creating supporting infrastructure
- **Customer Focus**: Understanding and meeting customer needs
- **Scalability**: Building systems for growth
- **Innovation**: Continuous improvement and development

### 3.7 Summary

Creating a compelling business design involves developing a clear vision, mission statement, value proposition, and business model. These elements work together to define the company's purpose, direction, and approach to creating value for customers. Successful entrepreneurs carefully craft these elements to align with customer needs, market opportunities, and competitive dynamics.

The business design process is iterative and requires continuous validation and refinement. Entrepreneurs must be willing to test their assumptions, learn from feedback, and adapt their business design as they gain market knowledge and experience.

Key takeaways include:
- Vision provides long-term direction
- Mission defines purpose and objectives
- Value proposition articulates unique value
- Business model describes value creation and capture
- Innovation is necessary in challenging markets
- Continuous validation and refinement are essential

The business design forms the foundation for entrepreneurial success and guides all subsequent business decisions and activities.
