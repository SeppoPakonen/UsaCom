# Chapter 11: Unique Marketing Issues

## SELECTING A MARKET AND ESTABLISHING A POSITION

Selecting the right market and establishing a strong position is critical for entrepreneurial success. This process involves several key steps:

### Market Segmentation

Market segmentation involves dividing a market into distinct groups of customers with similar characteristics and needs.

#### Demographic Segmentation
Dividing markets based on demographic variables such as:
- Age
- Gender
- Income
- Education
- Occupation
- Family size
- Life cycle stage

#### Geographic Segmentation
Dividing markets based on geographic criteria such as:
- Region
- Country
- City size
- Climate
- Population density

#### Psychographic Segmentation
Dividing markets based on psychological characteristics such as:
- Lifestyle
- Personality
- Values
- Attitudes
- Interests

#### Behavioral Segmentation
Dividing markets based on behavioral variables such as:
- Benefits sought
- Usage rate
- Loyalty status
- Purchase occasion
- User status

### Target Market Selection

After segmenting the market, entrepreneurs must select which segments to target based on:

#### Segment Size and Growth
- Market size and potential
- Growth rate and trends
- Future opportunities

#### Segment Structure
- Competitive intensity
- Supplier power
- Buyer power
- Threat of substitutes

#### Company Objectives and Resources
- Strategic objectives
- Available resources
- Company capabilities
- Risk tolerance

### Establishing a Unique Position

Positioning involves creating a distinct image and identity in customers' minds.

#### Positioning Strategies
- Attribute-based positioning
- Benefit-based positioning
- User-based positioning
- Competitor-based positioning
- Product category positioning

#### Positioning Process
1. Identify competitive advantages
2. Select positioning strategy
3. Develop positioning statement
4. Communicate position effectively

## KEY MARKETING ISSUES FOR NEW VENTURES

New ventures face unique marketing challenges that differ from established companies.

### Limited Resources

New ventures typically have limited marketing budgets, requiring creative and cost-effective approaches.

#### Strategies for Limited Budgets
- Focus on high-impact, low-cost tactics
- Leverage social media and digital marketing
- Build partnerships for shared marketing costs
- Focus on word-of-mouth and referral marketing
- Concentrate on specific target segments

### Unknown Brand

New ventures lack brand recognition, making it harder to attract customers.

#### Building Brand Awareness
- Consistent messaging and visual identity
- Content marketing and thought leadership
- Public relations and media outreach
- Customer testimonials and case studies
- Participation in industry events

### Limited Customer Base

New ventures start with few or no customers, requiring aggressive customer acquisition.

#### Customer Acquisition Strategies
- Free trials and samples
- Referral programs
- Influencer partnerships
- Direct sales efforts
- Online marketing campaigns

### SELLING BENEFITS RATHER THAN FEATURES

Successful marketing focuses on the benefits customers receive rather than product features.

#### Feature vs. Benefit
- Features describe what a product does
- Benefits describe what customers gain
- Customers buy benefits, not features

#### Translating Features to Benefits
- Identify customer needs and problems
- Connect features to customer benefits
- Use customer language and terminology
- Focus on outcomes and results

### Establishing a Brand

Branding is particularly important for new ventures to differentiate themselves from competitors.

#### Brand Elements
- Brand name
- Logo and visual identity
- Slogan or tagline
- Brand personality
- Brand values

#### Brand Building Strategies
- Consistent messaging across all touchpoints
- Deliver on brand promises
- Engage with customers authentically
- Create memorable experiences
- Build brand communities

### THE 4Ps OF MARKETING FOR NEW VENTURES

The traditional marketing mix applies to new ventures but with unique considerations.

#### Product
For new ventures, product development must balance:
- Customer needs and preferences
- Technical feasibility
- Market timing
- Resource constraints
- Competitive positioning

#### Price
Pricing strategies for new ventures include:
- Penetration pricing to gain market share
- Skimming pricing to maximize early profits
- Competitive pricing to match competitors
- Value-based pricing based on customer value
- Psychological pricing for perception

#### Promotion
Promotional strategies for new ventures focus on:
- Building awareness cost-effectively
- Generating trial and adoption
- Creating buzz and excitement
- Establishing credibility
- Building relationships

#### Place (or Distribution)
Distribution strategies for new ventures include:
- Direct sales to customers
- Online sales channels
- Retail partnerships
- Wholesale distribution
- Channel partnerships

### SAVVY ENTREPRENEURIAL FIRM: Utilizing Guerrilla Marketing Techniques to Create Product Awareness in Entertaining, Clever, and Effective Ways

Guerrilla marketing involves unconventional, low-cost marketing tactics that create memorable experiences.

#### Guerrilla Marketing Tactics
- Street marketing
- Ambient advertising
- Viral marketing
- Event marketing
- Cause marketing

#### Advantages
- Low cost
- High impact
- Memorable experiences
- Word-of-mouth generation
- Creative expression

#### Considerations
- Must align with brand
- Requires creativity and planning
- May not reach mass markets
- Potential for negative reactions
- Regulatory considerations

The key is to create marketing campaigns that are cost-effective, memorable, and aligned with the company's objectives and target market.
