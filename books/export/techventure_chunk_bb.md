# Technology Ventures: From Idea to Enterprise (continued)

## Chapter 26: Glossary of Terms

### Alphabetical Index of Key Terms

#### A

**Acquisition** - The purchase of one company by another. In the context of entrepreneurship, this often represents an exit strategy for startups.

**Angel Investor** - A wealthy individual who provides capital for a business startup, usually in exchange for convertible debt or ownership equity.

**Annuity Revenue Model** - A business model where customers pay a recurring fee at regular intervals for continued access to a product or service.

**Asset Sale** - A type of business sale where the buyer acquires specific assets of the company rather than its stock.

**Asymmetric Competition** - A competitive situation where competitors have different strengths, weaknesses, and strategies.

#### B

**Balanced Scorecard** - A strategic planning and management system that identifies and aligns business activities with vision and strategy.

**Barriers to Entry** - Obstacles that prevent new competitors from entering a market.

**Baseline** - A standard or reference point against which measurements or comparisons are made.

**Batch Production** - A manufacturing method where identical or similar items are produced together in groups.

**Behavioral Segmentation** - Dividing a market into groups based on consumer behavior, usage patterns, or decision-making approaches.

**Benchmarking** - The process of comparing business processes and performance metrics to industry bests or best practices from other companies.

#### C

**Cash Flow** - The movement of money into and out of a business during a specific period.

**Cash Flow Statement** - A financial statement that shows how changes in balance sheet accounts and income affect cash and cash equivalents.

**Cause Marketing** - A type of corporate marketing that links a company and its products/services to a social cause or issue.

**Channel** - The method or pathway through which a company communicates with customer segments and delivers a value proposition.

**Channel Conflict** - Disagreement or tension between different distribution channels.

**Closed Innovation** - A model where firms depend entirely on their own research to generate new ideas.

**Cloud Computing** - The delivery of computing services over the internet ("the cloud").

**Co-creation** - The process of creating value with customers rather than for customers.

**Code of Conduct** - A set of rules outlining the norms, rules, and responsibilities of, and proper practices for, an individual.

**Collaborative Innovation Network (COIN)** - A network of people who work together to achieve innovation.

**Commercialization** - The process of introducing a new product or production method into the market.

**Commoditization** - The process by which goods or services become commoditized, meaning they become indistinguishable from competing offerings.

**Competition** - The rivalry between companies trying to achieve the same business objectives.

**Competitive Advantage** - An advantage that a firm has over its competitors, allowing it to generate greater sales or margins.

**Competitive Analysis** - A strategy that identifies and evaluates competitors' strengths and weaknesses relative to one's own product or service.

**Competitive Intelligence** - The systematic collection and analysis of information regarding competitors and the competitive environment.

**Competitive Strategy** - A long-term plan of action that a company devises to gain a competitive advantage over its rivals.

**Complementary Assets** - Assets that are needed to derive value from a competitive advantage.

**Compound Annual Growth Rate (CAGR)** - The mean annual growth rate of an investment over a specified time period longer than one year.

**Concurrent Engineering** - A work methodology based on project management principles that emphasizes the parallelization of tasks.

**Conjoint Analysis** - A statistical technique used in market research that helps determine how people value different attributes.

**Consortium** - An association of two or more individuals, companies, or governments with the objective of participating in a common activity.

**Constant Returns to Scale** - A characteristic of a production process in which an increase in the scale of production has no effect on returns.

**Consumer Market** - A market where individuals purchase products and services for personal use.

**Consumer-Generated Media** - Content created by consumers rather than companies.

**Content Marketing** - A marketing strategy focused on creating and distributing valuable, relevant, and consistent content.

**Continuing Innovation** - An innovation that makes relatively minor changes to an existing product.

**Continuous Innovation** - An innovation that creates a new product category that is not a significant departure from existing products.

**Contract Manufacturing** - An arrangement in which one company provides part or all of the manufacturing services for another company.

**Contribution Margin** - The selling price per unit minus the variable cost per unit.

**Convenience Store** - A small retail business that stocks a range of everyday items and is often open at hours convenient for customers.

**Co-opetition** - A strategy where companies cooperate and compete at the same time.

**Cooperative** - A business organization owned and operated by a group of individuals for their shared benefit.

**Core Competency** - A harmonized combination of multiple resources and skills that distinguish a firm in the marketplace.

**Corporate Entrepreneurship** - The process of pursuing entrepreneurial opportunities within established companies.

**Corporate Venture Capital** - A strategic investment arm of a large corporation that invests in external startups.

**Cost Leadership** - A strategy that focuses on becoming the lowest-cost producer in the industry.

**Cost-Plus Pricing** - A pricing method used by companies to maximize their profits.

**Cost Structure** - All costs incurred to operate a business model.

**Crowdfunding** - The practice of funding a project or venture by raising small amounts of money from a large number of people.

**Crowdsourcing** - The practice of obtaining information or input into a task or project by enlisting the services of a large number of people.

**Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)** - The cost associated with acquiring a new customer.

**Customer Development** - A methodology for discovering and validating customer problems and solutions before building products.

**Customer Discovery** - The first step in customer development where entrepreneurs test their initial hypotheses about customers, problems, and solutions.

**Customer Development Process** - A methodology for discovering and validating customer problems and solutions before building products.

**Customer Lifetime Value (CLV)** - The total worth to a business of a customer over the whole period of their relationship.

**Customer Validation** - The second step in customer development where entrepreneurs prove that the business model is repeatable and scalable.

#### D

**Debt Financing** - When a company raises money by selling debt instruments to investors.

**Decision Tree** - A decision support tool that uses a tree-like graph or model of decisions and their possible consequences.

**Default Case** - A scenario that assumes the most likely outcome in a business plan.

**Defensive Strategy** - A strategy designed to protect a company's market position from competitive threats.

**Deflation** - A decrease in the general price level of goods and services.

**Delphi Method** - A structured communication technique that relies on a panel of experts.

**Demographic Segmentation** - Dividing a market into groups based on demographic variables such as age, gender, family size, income, etc.

**Demonstration** - A method of marketing that involves showing the product in use.

**Density** - The number of competitors in a specific market segment.

**Dependent Variable** - A variable that depends on other factors.

**Derivative Innovation** - An innovation that builds on existing products or technologies.

**Design for Manufacturing (DFM)** - The process of designing a product to facilitate manufacturing.

**Design Thinking** - An iterative process in which designers seek to understand users, empathize with them, and design solutions.

**Differential Innovation** - An innovation that creates a new product category that is a significant departure from existing products.

**Differentiation** - A strategy that focuses on creating unique products or services that command premium prices.

**Diffusion of Innovation** - The process by which innovation spreads.

**Direct Competition** - Competition from companies offering similar products or services to the same target market.

**Direct Investment** - An investment in which the investor acquires a measure of control in the enterprise.

**Direct Sales** - Selling products directly to consumers without intermediaries.

**Discontinuous Innovation** - An innovation that creates a new product category that is a significant departure from existing products.

**Discount Rate** - The interest rate used to determine the present value of future cash flows.

**Disruptive Innovation** - An innovation that creates a new market and value network that eventually disrupts an existing market.

**Distributors** - Companies that purchase products from manufacturers and sell them to retailers or end customers.

**Diversification** - A strategy that involves entering new markets with new products.

**Dividend** - A payment made by a corporation to its shareholders, usually as a distribution of profits.

**Domain Name** - A string of characters that identifies a particular IP address.

**Down Round** - A round of financing where the valuation of the company is lower than the previous round.

**Due Diligence** - An investigation or audit of a potential investment.

#### E

**E-commerce** - Commercial transactions conducted electronically on the internet.

**Economic Moat** - A competitive advantage that is sustainable over time.

**Economies of Scale** - Cost advantages that enterprises obtain due to their scale of operation.

**Economies of Scope** - Cost advantages that result from a firm producing multiple products.

**Elasticity** - The measurement of how responsive an economic variable is to a change in another.

**Electronic Data Interchange (EDI)** - The electronic interchange of business information using a standardized format.

**Elements of Value** - The fundamental benefits that customers expect from a company.

**Elevator Pitch** - A brief, persuasive speech that sparks interest in what someone's doing.

**Emerging Market** - A developing nation's economy that is in the process of rapid growth and industrialization.

**Emerging Technology** - A technology that currently exists but has not yet been widely adopted.

**Employee Stock Ownership Plan (ESOP)** - An employee benefit plan that provides employees with ownership interest in the company.

**Employment Agreement** - A contract between an employer and employee that outlines the terms of employment.

**End User** - The person who ultimately uses a product.

**End-of-Life Strategy** - A plan for managing a product at the end of its useful life.

**Entrepreneur** - A person who starts and operates a business, taking on greater financial risks to do so.

**Entrepreneurial Alertness** - The ability to notice opportunities without actively searching for them.

**Entrepreneurial Ecosystem** - A set of interconnected elements that create and support entrepreneurial activity.

**Entrepreneurial Finance** - The study of value creation and risk management in entrepreneurial ventures.

**Entrepreneurial Opportunity** - A business situation that creates value for the customer, entrepreneur, and other stakeholders.

**Entrepreneurial Orientation** - A set of processes, practices, and decision-making styles that are entrepreneurial in nature.

**Entrepreneurship** - The process of designing, launching, and running a new business.

**Entry Barriers** - Obstacles that prevent new competitors from entering a market.

**Equity Financing** - When a firm raises money by offering shares of ownership in the company to investors.

**Equity Incentive Plan** - A compensation plan that provides employees with ownership interest in the company.

**Equity Investment** - An investment in which the investor purchases equity in the company.

**Equity Stake** - An ownership interest in a company.

**Escrow** - Money or property held by a third party during a transaction between two parties.

**Exit Strategy** - A strategic plan for leaving a business venture.

**Experience Curve** - The systematic relationship between the cumulative volume of production and unit cost.

**Expert Systems** - Computer systems that mimic the decision-making ability of a human expert.

**Explicit Knowledge** - Knowledge that can be readily articulated, codified, and transferred.

#### F

**Factor of Production** - The inputs used in the production of goods or services.

**Factory** - A building or set of buildings where goods are manufactured or assembled.

**Family Business** - A business in which decision-making is influenced by multiple generations of a family.

**Fast Follower** - A company that quickly adopts innovations pioneered by others.

**Feasibility Analysis** - A preliminary, quick, and systematic study of a business idea to determine whether it has potential.

**Feature** - A distinctive attribute or aspect of a product or service.

**Federal Reserve** - The central banking system of the United States.

**Feedback** - Information about reactions to a product, a person's performance of a task, etc.

**Fiduciary** - A person who holds a legal or ethical relationship of trust with one or more other parties.

**Field Testing** - Testing a product or service in its intended environment.

**Financial Forecast** - A forecast of a company's future financial performance.

**Financial Institution** - A company engaged in the business of dealing with financial and monetary transactions.

**Financial Intermediary** - An institution that transfers funds from savers to borrowers.

**Financial Plan** - A comprehensive plan that includes all financial aspects of a business.

**Financial Risk** - The possibility of losing money on an investment or business venture.

**Financing** - The process of providing funds for business activities, making purchases, or investing.

**First-Mover Advantage** - The competitive advantage gained by the initial significant occupant of a market segment.

**First-Mover Disadvantage** - The disadvantages faced by the first company to enter a market.

**Fishbone Diagram** - A visualization tool for categorizing the potential causes of a problem.

**Flash Market** - A market characterized by rapid changes and short product life cycles.

**Focus Group** - A research method for gathering information about people's opinions through guided discussion.

**Follow-on Investment** - Additional investment in a company after the initial investment.

**Forecast** - A prediction of future events based on past and present data.

**Forecasting** - The process of making predictions about future events based on past and present data.

**Friction** - Factors that impede the flow of transactions or interactions.

**Frugal Innovation** - The process of reducing the complexity and cost of a good and its production.

**Funding** - The provision of money to finance a project, business, or other venture.

**Funding Round** - A stage in which a company raises money from investors.

**Funding Sources** - The various sources from which a company can raise capital.

**Funding Strategy** - A plan for how a company will raise capital.

**Funding Target** - The amount of money a company aims to raise in a funding round.

**Funding Vehicle** - The legal structure used to raise capital.

**Fungible** - Interchangeable, as in the case of goods or commodities of the same type and quality.

**Future Value** - The value of an asset or cash at a specified date in the future.

#### G

**Gambler's Ruin** - A statistical concept that states that a player with finite wealth will eventually go broke when playing a fair game.

**Game Theory** - The study of mathematical models of strategic interaction among rational decision-makers.

**Gantt Chart** - A type of bar chart that illustrates a project schedule.

**Gazelle** - A company that achieves rapid growth in terms of sales and employment.

**Genetic Algorithm** - A search heuristic that mimics the process of natural selection.

**Geographic Segmentation** - Dividing a market into different geographical units.

**Global Strategy** - A strategy that companies use to compete worldwide.

**Going Concern** - An accounting assumption that a business will continue to operate indefinitely.

**Golden Handshake** - A clause in an employment contract that provides a significant severance package.

**Golden Parachute** - A clause in an employment contract that provides benefits if the employee loses their job.

**Goodwill** - An intangible asset that arises when a buyer acquires an existing business.

**Government Contract** - A contract between a government agency and a private party.

**Government Grant** - A financial award by the government for a specific project or purpose.

**Government Regulation** - Rules and regulations created by government agencies.

**Grandfather Clause** - An exception that allows an old rule to continue to apply to some existing situations.

**Growth Hacking** - A marketing technique that focuses on growth using creative, low-cost strategies.

**Growth Rate** - The percentage change of a specific variable within a specific time period.

**Growth Strategy** - A plan for expanding a business.

**Guerilla Marketing** - An advertisement strategy that focuses on low-cost, creative methods.

#### H

**Hacker** - A person who uses computers to gain unauthorized access to data.

**Halo Effect** - The tendency for positive impressions of a person, company, or brand in one area.

**Hard Asset** - A tangible asset such as real estate, equipment, or inventory.

**Harvest Strategy** - A strategy that focuses on maximizing short-term cash flow.

**Hedging** - An investment position that offsets potential losses or gains.

**Herd Behavior** - When individuals in a group follow what others are doing.

**High-Touch** - A service approach that involves direct, personal interaction with customers.

**Holding Company** - A company that owns other companies' outstanding stock.

**Horizontal Integration** - The joining of two firms that are in the same industry.

**Human Capital** - The knowledge, skills, and experience possessed by an individual or population.

**Human Resources** - The department of a business that deals with employee-related matters.

**Hurdle Rate** - The minimum rate of return on a project or investment required by a manager.

**Hypertext Markup Language (HTML)** - The standard markup language for documents designed to be displayed in a web browser.

**Hypothetical Deduction** - A method of reasoning by which inferences are drawn from assumptions.
