#### Analysis of Investment Trends

##### Investment Volume Trends
- **Steady Growth**: From 2003-2008, investment volume grew steadily from $26.3B to $41.7B
- **2008 Peak**: Investment peaked in 2008 at $41.7B despite financial crisis
- **Post-Crisis Recovery**: Investment recovered to $39.7B in 2010 and $56.1B in 2011
- **2012 Decline**: Investment declined to $50.3B in 2012 despite increased deal volume

##### Deal Volume Trends
- **Consistent Deal Volume**: Deal volume remained relatively stable between 4,000-7,000 deals annually
- **Highest Volume**: 2012 had highest deal volume at 6,813 deals
- **Lowest Volume**: 2009 had lowest deal volume at 4,130 deals during financial crisis
- **Recovery**: Deal volume recovered to pre-crisis levels by 2010

##### IPO Activity Trends
- **2007 Peak**: IPO activity peaked in 2007 with 83 IPOs
- **2008 Crisis**: IPO activity dropped dramatically to 9 IPOs in 2008
- **Recovery**: IPO activity recovered to 65 IPOs in 2010 and 2012
- **Offering Amounts**: IPO offering amounts showed more volatility than deal counts

### 18.11 Due Diligence Process

#### Overview of Due Diligence
Due diligence is the process by which potential investors investigate a company before making an investment decision. It involves comprehensive analysis of the business, financials, technology, market, and management team.

##### Purpose of Due Diligence
- **Risk Assessment**: Identify and assess investment risks
- **Value Validation**: Validate company valuation
- **Assumption Testing**: Test business model assumptions
- **Opportunity Evaluation**: Evaluate investment opportunity
- **Investment Decision**: Support investment decision-making
- **Investment Terms**: Determine appropriate investment terms

##### Due Diligence Participants
- **Venture Capitalists**: Lead the due diligence process
- **Industry Experts**: Provide industry expertise
- **Technical Experts**: Evaluate technology and products
- **Financial Analysts**: Analyze financial statements and projections
- **Legal Counsel**: Review legal and regulatory issues
- **Accounting Firms**: Perform financial audits and analysis

#### Due Diligence Components

##### Business Model Analysis
- **Value Proposition**: Evaluate the company's value proposition
- **Revenue Model**: Analyze the revenue model and sustainability
- **Market Position**: Assess market position and competitive landscape
- **Growth Strategy**: Evaluate growth strategy and execution plan
- **Scalability**: Assess scalability of the business model
- **Risk Factors**: Identify key business risks

##### Financial Analysis
- **Historical Performance**: Analyze historical financial performance
- **Financial Projections**: Evaluate financial projections and assumptions
- **Cash Flow Analysis**: Assess cash flow generation and needs
- **Unit Economics**: Analyze unit economics and profitability
- **Financial Controls**: Evaluate financial controls and reporting
- **Audit Quality**: Assess quality of financial audits

##### Market Analysis
- **Market Size**: Evaluate total addressable market size
- **Market Growth**: Assess market growth rates and trends
- **Competitive Analysis**: Analyze competitive landscape
- **Customer Analysis**: Evaluate customer base and needs
- **Market Position**: Assess company's market position
- **Market Risks**: Identify market-related risks

##### Technology Assessment
- **Technology Evaluation**: Assess technology capabilities
- **IP Analysis**: Evaluate intellectual property portfolio
- **Technical Team**: Assess technical team capabilities
- **Development Process**: Evaluate development processes
- **Technology Risks**: Identify technology-related risks
- **Innovation Pipeline**: Assess innovation pipeline

##### Management Evaluation
- **Team Assessment**: Evaluate management team capabilities
- **Track Record**: Assess management track record
- **Skills Assessment**: Evaluate required skills and experience
- **Leadership**: Assess leadership capabilities
- **Commitment**: Evaluate management commitment

##### Legal and Regulatory Review
- **Corporate Structure**: Review corporate structure and governance
- **Legal Issues**: Identify potential legal issues

- **Litigation**: Identify any pending litigation

### 18.12 Investment Terms and Valuation

#### Key Investment Terms

##### Valuation
- **Pre-Money Valuation**: Company value before investment
- **Post-Money Valuation**: Company value after investment
- **Liquidation Preferences**: Priority in liquidation events
- **Anti-Dilution**: Protection against down rounds
- **Voting Rights**: Voting rights for investors
- **Board Representation**: Board seats for investors

##### Control Provisions
- **Board Composition**: Board composition and voting rights
- **Protective Provisions**: Investor veto rights on key decisions
- **Information Rights**: Access to company information
- **Registration Rights**: Rights to register shares for public sale
- **Tag-Along Rights**: Rights to participate in sales
- **Drag-Along Rights**: Rights to force other shareholders to sell

##### Financial Provisions
- **Dividends**: Dividend rights and preferences
- **Liquidation Preferences**: Priority in liquidation events
- **Anti-Dilution**: Protection against down rounds
- **Conversion Rights**: Rights to convert preferred to common stock
- **Redemption Rights**: Rights to require company to repurchase shares
- **Participation Rights**: Rights to participate in distributions

#### Valuation Methods

##### Market-Based Methods
- **Comparable Company Analysis**: Compare to similar public companies
- **Precedent Transaction Analysis**: Compare to similar transactions
- **Market Multiples**: Apply market multiples to financial metrics
- **Revenue Multiples**: Apply revenue multiples
- **EBITDA Multiples**: Apply EBITDA multiples
- **Market Comparisons**: Compare to market valuations

##### Income-Based Methods
- **Discounted Cash Flow**: Discount projected cash flows
- **Net Present Value**: Calculate NPV of future cash flows
- **Terminal Value**: Calculate terminal value of business
- **Discount Rate**: Apply appropriate discount rate
- **Growth Assumptions**: Make growth assumptions
- **Risk Adjustment**: Adjust for risk factors

##### Asset-Based Methods
- **Book Value**: Use book value of assets
- **Adjusted Book Value**: Adjust book value for market value
- **Liquidation Value**: Calculate liquidation value
- **Replacement Cost**: Calculate replacement cost of assets
- **Asset Quality**: Assess quality of assets
- **Asset Utilization**: Assess asset utilization

##### Venture Capital Method
- **Terminal Value**: Project terminal value at exit
- **Discount Rate**: Apply discount rate for risk
- **Ownership Calculation**: Calculate required ownership
- **Investment Amount**: Determine investment amount
- **Exit Timeline**: Project exit timeline
- **Return Expectations**: Factor in return expectations

### 18.14 Summary

Raising capital from external investors is a critical milestone for technology ventures. The process involves understanding investor requirements, preparing comprehensive materials, and negotiating appropriate terms. Success requires demonstrating market potential, management capability, and clear path to returns.

Key takeaways include:
- Venture capital investors seek companies with strong market potential
- Due diligence is a comprehensive evaluation process
- Investment terms significantly impact ownership and control
- Post-investment support adds significant value
- Exit planning should begin early in the investment process
- Professional relationships are critical for success

The venture capital process is complex and requires careful preparation and execution. Entrepreneurs who understand investor perspectives and prepare thoroughly are more likely to secure favorable terms and achieve successful outcomes.
