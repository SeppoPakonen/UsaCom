## PATENTS

Patents are legal rights granted to inventors for their inventions, providing exclusive rights to make, use, and sell the invention for a limited time period.

### Types of Patents

#### Utility Patents
Utility patents protect new and useful processes, machines, articles of manufacture, or compositions of matter. These are the most common type of patent for business innovations.

#### Design Patents
Design patents protect the ornamental design of an article of manufacture. They cover the appearance rather than the function of an invention.

#### Plant Patents
Plant patents protect new varieties of plants that have been asexually reproduced.

### Requirements for Patent Protection

#### Novelty
The invention must be new and not previously disclosed in any prior publication or use.

#### Non-Obviousness
The invention must not be obvious to someone with ordinary skill in the relevant field.

#### Utility
The invention must have a useful purpose and be operable.

#### Patentable Subject Matter
The invention must fall within the categories of patentable subject matter defined by law.

### Who Can Apply for a Patent?

#### Inventors
The actual inventors are entitled to apply for patents.

#### Employers
In some cases, employers may own inventions made by employees in the course of employment.

#### Assignees
Patent rights can be transferred to other parties through assignment.

### The Process of Obtaining a Patent

#### Patent Search
Conducting a search to determine if similar inventions already exist.

#### Patent Application Preparation
Preparing a detailed description of the invention and claims defining the scope of protection sought.

#### Filing the Application
Submitting the application to the patent office with required fees.

#### Prosecution
Responding to office actions and communications from the patent examiner.

#### Patent Issuance
Receiving the patent grant after successful prosecution.

#### Maintenance
Paying maintenance fees to keep the patent in force.

### Patent Infringement

Patent infringement occurs when someone makes, uses, sells, or imports a patented invention without permission.

#### Enforcement Options
- Cease and desist letters
- Lawsuits for damages
- Injunctive relief
- Settlement negotiations

#### Defenses to Infringement
- Invalidity of the patent
- Non-infringement
- Patent misuse
- Prior use rights
- Experimental use

#### Avoiding Infringement
- Freedom to operate searches
- Design-around strategies
- Licensing agreements
- Legal opinions

## TRADEMARKS

Trademarks protect words, phrases, symbols, or designs that identify and distinguish the source of goods or services.

### The Four Types of Trademarks

#### Word Marks
Words or combinations of words that identify and distinguish goods or services.

#### Design Marks
Logos, symbols, or other graphical elements that serve as source identifiers.

#### Composite Marks
Combinations of words and designs.

#### Trade Dress
The overall appearance and image of a product or its packaging.

### What Is Protected Under Trademark Law?

#### Source Identification
Trademarks identify the source of goods or services.

#### Quality Assurance
Trademarks assure consumers of consistent quality.

#### Marketing Tool
Trademarks serve as marketing tools that build brand recognition.

#### Exclusive Rights
Trademark owners have exclusive rights to use the mark in connection with their goods or services.

### Exclusions from Trademark Protection

#### Generic Terms
Terms that have become generic for the goods or services.

#### Descriptive Terms
Terms that merely describe the goods or services (unless they've acquired distinctiveness).

#### Functional Features
Features that are essential to the use or purpose of the goods or their manufacture.

#### Government Symbols
Official symbols, flags, or names.

### The Process of Obtaining a Trademark

#### Trademark Search
Searching to ensure the mark is available and doesn't conflict with existing marks.

#### Use in Commerce
Using the mark in connection with goods or services in commerce.

#### Federal Registration
Filing an application with the trademark office for federal protection.

#### Maintenance
Continuing use and filing required maintenance documents.

### Advantages of Federal Registration

#### Nationwide Rights
Federal registration provides nationwide rights in the mark.

#### Legal Presumptions
Registered marks receive legal presumptions in enforcement actions.

#### Exclusive Rights
Registration provides exclusive rights to use the mark.

#### Enforcement Tools
Registration provides enhanced enforcement tools.

#### International Protection
Registration facilitates international protection.
