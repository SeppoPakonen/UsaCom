### WHAT WENT WRONG? Burberry: How a Traditional British Brand Lost Its Favorable Position in the Fashion Industry

This section examines how Burberry, a traditional British luxury brand, lost its favorable position in the fashion industry. The case illustrates the importance of understanding industry dynamics and adapting to changing market conditions.

Burberry's decline was caused by several factors:

#### Brand Dilution
Burberry expanded its product line and licensing agreements too rapidly, which diluted the brand's exclusivity and prestige. The company licensed its brand to numerous product categories, making it less distinctive.

#### Counterfeiting Issues
The brand became associated with counterfeit goods, which damaged its reputation and reduced its appeal to luxury consumers.

#### Failure to Adapt
Burberry failed to adapt to changing consumer preferences and market trends, maintaining traditional approaches when the industry was evolving.

#### Competition from New Entrants
New luxury brands emerged that better captured the attention of younger consumers and offered more contemporary designs.

The Burberry case highlights the importance of:
- Maintaining brand integrity during expansion
- Protecting intellectual property
- Adapting to market changes
- Understanding competitive dynamics
- Preserving brand value

### The Five Competitive Forces Model

Porter's Five Forces Model is a framework for analyzing the competitive environment of an industry. The five forces are:

#### 1. Threat of New Entrants
This force examines how easy or difficult it is for new competitors to enter the market. Factors include:
- Entry barriers (capital requirements, economies of scale, brand loyalty)
- Government regulation
- Access to distribution channels
- Proprietary technology or resources

#### 2. Bargaining Power of Suppliers
This force analyzes the power suppliers have to drive up prices. Factors include:
- Number of suppliers
- Uniqueness of supplier products
- Switching costs for the industry
- Supplier concentration

#### 3. Bargaining Power of Buyers
This force examines the power buyers have to drive down prices. Factors include:
- Number of buyers
- Buyer concentration
- Switching costs
- Price sensitivity
- Buyer information

#### 4. Threat of Substitute Products
This force looks at alternative products that can satisfy the same customer need. Factors include:
- Availability of substitutes
- Relative price performance of substitutes
- Switching costs
- Customer willingness to substitute

#### 5. Rivalry Among Existing Competitors
This force examines the intensity of competition among existing firms. Factors include:
- Number and size of competitors
- Industry growth rate
- Fixed costs
- Product differentiation
- Exit barriers

### SAVVY ENTREPRENEURIAL FIRM: How the Bargaining Power of Buyers Changed the Fate of Sony in Its Start-Up Years (continued)

Sony's early success was also influenced by understanding the bargaining power of different buyer segments. The company recognized that:

- Early adopters had lower price sensitivity and higher quality expectations
- Corporate buyers prioritized reliability and support over price
- Consumer segments varied in their preferences and purchasing power

By segmenting buyers based on their bargaining power and preferences, Sony could tailor its offerings and pricing strategies accordingly.

### The Value of the Five Forces Model

The Five Forces Model provides entrepreneurs with several benefits:

#### Strategic Planning
The model helps entrepreneurs understand the competitive landscape and identify strategic opportunities.

#### Risk Assessment
By analyzing all five forces, entrepreneurs can identify potential threats and challenges.

#### Market Entry Decisions
The model helps determine whether an industry is attractive for new entrants.

#### Competitive Positioning
Understanding the forces helps entrepreneurs position their ventures effectively.

#### Resource Allocation
The analysis guides decisions about where to invest resources for maximum competitive advantage.

### Industry Types and the Opportunities They Offer

Different industry types offer varying opportunities for entrepreneurs:

#### Fragmented Industries
Fragmented industries have many small competitors with no dominant player. Opportunities include:
- Consolidation through acquisition
- Service differentiation
- Geographic expansion
- Niche market focus

#### Emerging Industries
Emerging industries are in early stages of development. Opportunities include:
- First-mover advantages
- Standard-setting opportunities
- Market education
- Technology leadership

#### Mature Industries
Mature industries have established players and slow growth. Opportunities include:
- Process improvements
- Cost reduction
- Market segmentation
- International expansion

#### Declining Industries
Declining industries face shrinking demand. Opportunities include:
- Market consolidation
- Cost leadership
- Niche specialization
- International expansion

## COMPETITOR ANALYSIS

Competitor analysis is essential for understanding the competitive landscape and identifying opportunities for differentiation. A thorough competitor analysis includes:

### Identifying Competitors

The first step is identifying all potential competitors, including:

#### Direct Competitors
Companies offering similar products or services to the same target market.

#### Indirect Competitors
Companies offering different products or services that satisfy the same customer need.

#### Future Competitors
Companies that could become competitors in the future.

#### Substitute Products
Products or services that can replace the proposed offering.

### Sources of Competitive Intelligence

Entrepreneurs can gather competitive intelligence from various sources:

#### Public Information
- Company websites and annual reports

- Patent filings
- Job postings
- Social media presence

#### Customer Feedback
- Customer surveys and reviews
- Focus groups
- Direct customer interviews
- Mystery shopping

#### Industry Reports
- Market research reports
- Trade publications
- Industry conferences
- Professional associations

#### Direct Observation
- Visiting competitor locations
- Purchasing competitor products
- Attending competitor events
- Networking with former employees

### Completing a Competitive Analysis Grid

A competitive analysis grid helps organize information about competitors. The grid typically includes:

#### Key Success Factors
Critical elements that determine success in the industry.

#### Competitor Ratings
Assessment of how each competitor performs on each factor.

#### Strategic Implications
Analysis of competitive strengths and weaknesses.

#### Opportunity Identification
Areas where the new venture can differentiate itself.

The competitive analysis grid provides a systematic way to compare the proposed venture against existing competitors and identify opportunities for competitive advantage.
