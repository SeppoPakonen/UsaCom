## Customer Interface (continued)

### Revenue Streams
Revenue streams represent the cash a company generates from each customer segment. There are two main types of revenue streams:

#### Transaction-Based Revenue
Revenue generated from one-time customer payments, such as:
- Sales of products
- Service fees
- Rental fees
- Licensing fees
- Brokerage fees

#### Recurring Revenue
Revenue generated from ongoing payments from customers, such as:
- Subscription fees
- Usage fees
- Retainer fees
- Maintenance contracts
- Lease payments

### Pricing Mechanisms
The method a company uses to determine the price of its products or services:

#### Fixed Pricing
Standard pricing that doesn't change based on customer or market conditions.

#### Dynamic Pricing
Pricing that changes based on market demand, customer segment, or other factors.

#### Negotiated Pricing
Pricing that is determined through negotiation with individual customers.

#### Auction Pricing
Pricing determined through competitive bidding.

#### Yield Management
Pricing that varies based on capacity utilization and demand.

## INITITAL ETHICAL AND LEGAL ISSUES FACING A NEW FIRM

New firms face numerous ethical and legal issues that must be addressed from the beginning. These issues include:

### Establishing a Strong Ethical Culture for a Firm

Creating an ethical culture is essential for long-term success. Key elements include:

#### Leadership Commitment
Leaders must demonstrate ethical behavior and make ethics a priority.

#### Clear Values and Standards
The company should establish clear ethical values and standards that guide decision-making.

#### Training and Education
Employees should receive training on ethical standards and decision-making.

#### Accountability Systems
Mechanisms should be in place to hold employees accountable for ethical behavior.

#### Reporting Mechanisms
Employees should have safe ways to report ethical concerns.

#### Consistent Enforcement
Ethical standards must be enforced consistently across the organization.

### Choosing an Attorney for a Firm

Selecting the right attorney is crucial for new ventures. Considerations include:

#### Specialization
The attorney should specialize in business law and understand the specific needs of startups.

#### Experience
Experience with similar businesses and situations is valuable.

#### Cost Structure
Understanding the attorney's fee structure and budget implications.

#### Communication Style
The attorney should communicate effectively and be responsive to client needs.

#### Network and Connections
An attorney with relevant business connections can provide additional value.

### DRAFTING A FOUNDERS' AGREEMENT

A founders' agreement is essential for ventures with multiple founders. Key provisions include:

#### Ownership Percentages
Clear allocation of ownership percentages among founders.

#### Roles and Responsibilities
Definition of each founder's role and responsibilities.

#### Decision-Making Authority
Specification of decision-making authority and processes.

#### Vesting Schedules
Vesting arrangements for equity to ensure commitment.

#### Departure Provisions
Procedures for handling founder departures.

#### Intellectual Property Ownership
Clarification of intellectual property ownership.

#### Non-Compete and Non-Disclosure
Protection of company interests through appropriate restrictions.

### AVOIDING LEGAL DISPUTES

Prevention is better than litigation. Strategies for avoiding legal disputes include:

#### Clear Contracts
Well-drafted contracts that clearly define rights and obligations.

#### Regular Reviews
Regular review and updating of legal documents.

#### Communication
Open communication to address issues before they escalate.

#### Documentation
Thorough documentation of business decisions and agreements.

#### Professional Advice
Seeking professional legal advice before problems arise.

### WHAT WENT WRONG? How Legal Snafus Can Stop a Business in Its Tracks

This section examines cases where legal issues severely impacted or destroyed businesses. Common legal snafus include:

#### Inadequate Business Structure
Poor choice of business structure leading to liability or tax issues.

#### Contract Disputes
Poorly drafted or misunderstood contracts leading to disputes.

#### Intellectual Property Issues
Failure to protect or infringement of intellectual property rights.

#### Employment Law Violations
Violations of employment laws and regulations.

#### Regulatory Compliance
Failure to comply with industry regulations.

The lesson is that addressing legal issues proactively is far less costly than dealing with legal problems after they arise.
