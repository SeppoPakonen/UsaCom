## E8-9: Computing Depreciation under Alternative Methods (Continued)

Purity Ice Cream Company bought a new ice cream maker:
- Cost: $9,000
- Estimated useful life: 4 years
- Residual value: $1,000
- Estimated productive life: 16,000 hours
- Actual usage: Year 1: 5,500 hrs; Year 2: 3,800 hrs; Year 3: 3,200 hrs; Year 4: 3,500 hrs

**Required:** Complete a separate depreciation schedule for each method.

### a. Straight-Line Method

| Year | Computation | Depreciation Expense | Accumulated Depreciation | Net Book Value |
|------|-------------|---------------------|-------------------------|----------------|
| At acquisition | | | | $9,000 |
| 1 | ($9,000-$1,000)/4 | $2,000 | $2,000 | 7,000 |
| 2 | ($9,000-$1,000)/4 | 2,000 | 4,000 | 5,000 |
| 3 | ($9,000-$1,000)/4 | 2,000 | 6,000 | 3,000 |
| 4 | ($9,000-$1,000)/4 | 2,000 | 8,000 | 1,000 |

### b. Units-of-Production Method

Depreciation rate = ($9,000 - $1,000) / 16,000 = $0.50 per hour

| Year | Computation | Depreciation Expense | Accumulated Depreciation | Net Book Value |
|------|-------------|---------------------|-------------------------|----------------|
| At acquisition | | | | $9,000 |
| 1 | $0.50 × 5,500 | $2,750 | $2,750 | 6,250 |
| 2 | $0.50 × 3,800 | 1,900 | 4,650 | 4,350 |
| 3 | $0.50 × 3,200 | 1,600 | 6,250 | 2,750 |
| 4 | $0.50 × 3,500* | 1,750 | 8,000 | 1,000 |

*Note: Year 4 is limited to bring book value to residual value.

### c. Double-Declining-Balance Method

DDB rate = (1/4) × 2 = 50%

| Year | Computation | Depreciation Expense | Accumulated Depreciation | Net Book Value |
|------|-------------|---------------------|-------------------------|----------------|
| At acquisition | | | | $9,000 |
| 1 | $9,000 × 50% | $4,500 | $4,500 | 4,500 |
| 2 | $4,500 × 50% | 2,250 | 6,750 | 2,250 |
| 3 | $2,250 × 50% | 1,125 | 7,875 | 1,125 |
| 4 | Plug to residual | 125 | 8,000 | 1,000 |

### 2. Management Considerations

Assuming that the machine was used directly in the production of one of the products that the company manufactures and sells, what factors might management consider in selecting a preferable depreciation method in conformity with the expense principle?

**Answer:** Management should consider:
- **Matching principle**: The depreciation method should match the pattern of economic benefits consumed
- **Usage pattern**: If the machine's productivity declines with age, an accelerated method may be appropriate
- **Units-of-production**: Best if wear and tear is the primary factor in asset deterioration
- **Tax implications**: Different methods affect taxable income differently
- **Financial reporting**: Impact on reported earnings and financial ratios

## Explaining Depreciation Policy

### General Motors Corporation Note (2001 Annual Report)

**NOTE 3. SIGNIFICANT ACCOUNTING POLICIES**
**Property, Net**

> Property, plant, and equipment, including internal use software, is recorded at cost. Major improvements that extend the useful life of property are capitalized. Expenditures for repairs and maintenance are charged to expense as incurred. At January 1, 2001, we adopted the straight-line method of depreciation for real estate, facilities, and equipment placed in service after that date. Assets placed in service before January 1, 2001, continue to be depreciated using accelerated methods. The accelerated methods accumulate depreciation of approximately two-thirds of the depreciable cost in the first half of the estimated useful lives of property groups.

**Analysis:**

1. **Capital vs. Expense Policy:**
   - Capital expenditures: Major improvements that extend useful life
   - Revenue expenditures: Repairs and maintenance (expensed as incurred)

2. **Depreciation Method Change:**
   - Before 2001: Accelerated methods
   - After 2001: Straight-line for new assets
   - Old assets continue with accelerated methods (grandfathered)

3. **Accelerated Method Impact:**
   - Accumulates approximately 2/3 of depreciable cost in first half of useful life
   - Results in higher expenses and lower income in early years
   - Lower expenses and higher income in later years
