### Guidelines for Writing a Business Plan (continued)

#### Financial Projections

Financial projections are a critical component of any business plan. They should be realistic, well-researched, and clearly explained. Key elements include:

##### Revenue Projections
Revenue projections should be based on realistic assumptions about sales volume, pricing, and market penetration. These projections should include:

- Monthly projections for the first year
- Quarterly projections for years two and three
- Annual projections for years four and five
- Supporting assumptions and market research

##### Expense Projections
Expense projections should account for all costs associated with operating the business, including:

- Fixed costs (rent, insurance, salaries)
- Variable costs (materials, shipping, commissions)
- Seasonal variations
- Growth-related expenses

##### Cash Flow Projections
Cash flow projections are particularly important for new ventures, as they show the timing of money coming in and going out. Many profitable businesses fail due to poor cash flow management.

#### Start-up Costs

A detailed explanation of where the money will come from should be provided. Avoid cursory explanations such as "I plan to bring investors on board" or "I'll borrow the money." Although you may ultimately involve investors or lenders in your business, a more thoughtful account is required of how you'll provide for your initial cash needs. We'll cover funding and financing in Chapter 10.

If the money will come from friends and family or is raised through other means, such as credit cards or a home equity line of credit, a reasonable plan should be stipulated to repay the money. Showing how a new venture's start-up costs will be covered and repaid is an important issue. Many new ventures look promising as ongoing concerns but have no way of raising the money to get started or are never able to recover from the initial costs involved. When projecting start-up expenses, it is better to overestimate rather than underestimate the costs involved. Murphy's Law is prevalent in the start-up world—things will go wrong. It is a rare start-up that doesn't experience some unexpected expenses during the start-up phase.

There are worksheets posted online that help entrepreneurs determine the start-up costs to launch their respective businesses. Start-up cost worksheets are available via SCORE (www.score.org) and the Small Business Administration (www.sba.gov).

### OUTLINE OF THE BUSINESS PLAN

A well-structured business plan typically includes the following sections:

#### Executive Summary
The executive summary is the most important part of the business plan. It should be written last but placed first in the document. The executive summary should:

- Capture the reader's attention
- Summarize the key points of the plan
- Demonstrate the viability of the venture
- Be concise (typically 1-2 pages)

#### Company Description
This section provides detailed information about the company, including:

- Business concept and mission
- Legal structure and ownership
- History and background
- Location and facilities
- Products and services offered

#### Market Analysis
The market analysis section demonstrates understanding of the industry and target market. It should include:

- Industry size and growth trends
- Target market demographics and characteristics
- Market needs and opportunities
- Competitive analysis
- Market positioning strategy

#### Organization and Management
This section describes the company's organizational structure and management team:

- Organizational chart
- Management team bios
- Personnel plans
- Advisory board information
- Professional service providers

#### Service or Product Line
This section details the products or services offered:

- Product descriptions
- Research and development activities
- Manufacturing and production
- Quality control processes
- Future product plans

#### Marketing and Sales Strategy
This section outlines how the company will reach and serve customers:

- Marketing mix (product, price, place, promotion)
- Sales strategy and tactics
- Advertising and promotional plans
- Customer service approach
- Distribution channels

#### Financial Projections
This section provides detailed financial forecasts:

- Revenue and expense projections
- Cash flow statements
- Balance sheets
- Break-even analysis
- Funding requirements

#### Funding Request
If seeking funding, this section specifies the amount and terms:

- Total funding required
- Use of funds
- Proposed terms
- Future funding requirements
- Exit strategy for investors

#### Appendix
Supporting documents and additional information:

- Resumes of key personnel
- Market research data
- Legal documents
- Letters of intent from customers
- Technical specifications

### Exploring Each Section of the Plan

Each section of the business plan serves a specific purpose and should be developed with care. The plan should be tailored to the intended audience, whether it's potential investors, lenders, or internal management.

#### PARTNERING FOR SUCCESS: Types of Partnerships That are Common in Business Plans

This section explores various types of partnerships that entrepreneurs might consider including in their business plans:

##### Strategic Partnerships
Strategic partnerships involve collaboration between companies to achieve mutual benefits. These partnerships can provide:

- Access to new markets
- Shared resources and capabilities
- Reduced costs and risks
- Enhanced competitive position

##### Supplier Partnerships
Supplier partnerships involve close collaboration with key vendors to ensure quality, cost-effectiveness, and reliability. Benefits include:

- Priority treatment and allocation
- Volume discounts
- Customized solutions
- Collaborative innovation

##### Distributor Partnerships
Distributor partnerships help companies reach customers more effectively. These partnerships can provide:

- Market access and penetration
- Local market knowledge
- Sales and marketing support
- Customer service capabilities

##### Technology Partnerships
Technology partnerships involve collaboration on product development or technology integration. These partnerships can provide:

- Access to specialized expertise
- Shared development costs
- Faster time to market
- Enhanced product capabilities

### WHAT WENT WRONG? What the Enthusiast Group Learned About Assumptions, the Hard Way

This section examines how the Enthusiast Group made unrealistic assumptions in their business plan, leading to significant challenges. The case highlights the importance of:

- Grounding assumptions in market research
- Testing key assumptions before full implementation
- Building flexibility into plans
- Monitoring and adjusting based on feedback
- Avoiding overly optimistic projections

### PRESENTING THE BUSINESS PLAN TO INVESTORS

Presenting a business plan to investors requires careful preparation and execution. Key considerations include:

#### The Oral Presentation of a Business Plan

The oral presentation should complement the written plan and engage the audience. Best practices include:

- Clear, concise delivery
- Visual aids and supporting materials
- Confidence and enthusiasm
- Prepared responses to questions
- Respect for time limits

#### Questions and Feedback to Expect from Investors

Investors typically ask questions about:

- Market size and opportunity
- Competitive advantages
- Management team qualifications
- Financial projections and assumptions
- Exit strategy and returns
- Risk factors and mitigation
