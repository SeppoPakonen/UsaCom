# Chapter 3: Feasibility Analysis (continued)

## First Screen

Before conducting a detailed feasibility analysis, entrepreneurs should apply a "first screen" to quickly evaluate whether an idea is worth pursuing in detail. This preliminary evaluation should consider:

### Market Potential
- Is there a genuine need for the product or service?
- Is the target market large enough to support a viable business?
- Are customers willing to pay for the solution?

### Competitive Advantage
- Does the idea offer something unique or better than existing alternatives?
- Can the competitive advantage be sustained over time?
- Are there barriers to entry that protect the concept?

### Resource Requirements
- Are the necessary resources available or obtainable?
- Is the investment required proportional to the potential returns?
- Can the venture be launched with available resources?

### Risk Assessment
- What are the primary risks associated with the venture?
- Can these risks be mitigated or managed effectively?
- Is the level of risk acceptable given potential rewards?

## Chapter Summary

This chapter emphasized the importance of feasibility analysis in the entrepreneurial process. Key points include:

- Feasibility analysis determines whether a business idea has potential for success
- Four components comprise a comprehensive feasibility analysis
- Preliminary screening helps prioritize ideas worth detailed analysis
- Thorough analysis reduces risk before significant investment
- Poor feasibility analysis can lead to business failure

## You Be the VC 3.1

### The Opportunity

Sarah Kim developed a feasibility analysis for a subscription box service targeting busy professionals who want healthy, pre-prepared meals. Her analysis showed strong market demand, manageable startup costs, and favorable competitive dynamics.

### The Challenge

Despite positive feasibility results, Sarah faces challenges in supply chain management, customer acquisition costs, and market saturation with similar services.

### The Decision

As a venture capitalist, would you invest in Sarah's subscription box service based on her feasibility analysis? Consider operational challenges and market competition.

## You Be the VC 3.2

### The Opportunity

Robert Chen conducted a feasibility analysis for a mobile car detailing service. His analysis indicated strong demand from busy urban professionals, low startup costs, and minimal competition in his target market.

### The Challenge

The service requires significant time investment per customer, weather dependency, and difficulty scaling operations.

### The Decision

As a venture capitalist, would you invest in Robert's mobile car detailing service? Consider scalability and operational efficiency.
