## OBTAINING BUSINESS LICENSES AND PERMITS

New businesses must obtain appropriate licenses and permits to operate legally. Requirements vary by location and type of business.

### Business Licenses

Most jurisdictions require businesses to obtain licenses to operate legally. Common types include:

#### General Business License
Required by most cities for any business operating within city limits. Requirements and fees vary by location and type of business.

#### Professional Licenses
Required for certain professions such as doctors, lawyers, accountants, and contractors. These licenses ensure practitioners meet minimum competency standards.

#### Industry-Specific Licenses
Required for businesses in regulated industries such as banking, insurance, transportation, and healthcare.

#### Home Occupation Permits
Required for businesses operated from residential properties. These permits ensure the business doesn't negatively impact the neighborhood.

### Business Permits

Permits are typically required for specific activities or operations:

#### Building Permits
Required for construction, renovation, or modification of buildings or structures.

#### Signage Permits
Required for business signs, especially in commercial districts with specific regulations.

#### Health Department Permits
Required for businesses that handle food, provide health services, or pose potential health risks.

#### Fire Department Permits
Required for businesses that pose fire hazards or require special safety measures.

#### Environmental Permits
Required for businesses that may impact the environment or handle hazardous materials.

## CHOOSING A FORM OF BUSINESS ORGANIZATION

Selecting the appropriate form of business organization is one of the most important decisions for new ventures. The choice affects taxes, liability, ownership, and administrative requirements.

### Sole Proprietorship

A sole proprietorship is the simplest form of business organization, owned and operated by one person.

#### Advantages
- Easy and inexpensive to form
- Complete control by owner
- Minimal government regulation
- Direct receipt of profits
- Tax advantages (profits taxed only once)

#### Disadvantages
- Unlimited personal liability
- Difficulty raising capital
- Limited life of business
- Difficulty attracting high-caliber employees
- All responsibility falls on one person

#### When to Choose
Sole proprietorships are appropriate for:
- Small businesses with low risk
- Service businesses with minimal liability
- Businesses that don't need significant capital
- Owners who want complete control

### Partnerships

A partnership is a business owned by two or more people who share profits and losses.

#### General Partnership
In a general partnership, all partners share equally in management and liability.

##### Advantages
- Easy to form
- Shared management and expertise
- Shared financial burden
- Tax advantages (profits taxed only once)
- Combined skills and resources

##### Disadvantages
- Unlimited personal liability
- Potential for disagreements
- Shared profits
- Joint and several liability
- Limited life of partnership

#### Limited Partnership
A limited partnership includes both general partners (with unlimited liability) and limited partners (with limited liability).

##### Advantages
- Limited liability for limited partners
- Shared management and expertise
- Shared financial burden
- Tax advantages

##### Disadvantages
- Complex to form
- General partners have unlimited liability
- Limited partners have limited control
- Potential for disagreements

#### When to Choose
Partnerships are appropriate for:
- Businesses requiring multiple skills
- Ventures needing significant capital
- Situations where risk can be shared
- Professional services

### Corporations

A corporation is a separate legal entity owned by shareholders.

#### Closely Held Corporation
A corporation with a small number of shareholders, often family members or business associates.

#### Public Corporation
A corporation with shares traded on public stock exchanges.

#### Advantages
- Limited liability for shareholders
- Easy transfer of ownership
- Unlimited life
- Easier to raise capital
- Separate legal existence

#### Disadvantages
- Double taxation (profits taxed at corporate level and dividends taxed at shareholder level)
- Complex to form and maintain
- Significant government regulation
- Potential loss of control
- Higher costs of formation and operation

#### When to Choose
Corporations are appropriate for:
- Businesses seeking significant outside investment
- Ventures with high liability risk
- Companies planning to go public
- Businesses expecting to grow significantly

### Limited Liability Company

A limited liability company (LLC) combines the benefits of partnerships and corporations.

#### Advantages
- Limited liability for members
- Pass-through taxation (avoiding double taxation)
- Flexible management structure
- Fewer formalities than corporations
- Flexible profit distribution

#### Disadvantages
- More complex to form than partnerships
- Limited life in some states
- Potential self-employment tax issues
- Less certainty in legal precedents

#### When to Choose
LLCs are appropriate for:
- Small to medium-sized businesses
- Ventures wanting liability protection
- Businesses preferring pass-through taxation
- Situations requiring flexible management
