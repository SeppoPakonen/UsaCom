# Chapter 3: Feasibility Analysis

## FEASIBILITY ANALYSIS

Feasibility analysis is a preliminary, quick, and systematic study of a business idea to determine whether it has the potential to become a successful business venture. The purpose is to answer the question: "Does this business idea have a chance of success?" A feasibility analysis is different from a business plan. A business plan is a document that describes the strategy and implementation details for a new venture. A feasibility analysis is conducted before a business plan to determine whether the business plan is worth developing.

### Purpose of Feasibility Analysis

The primary purpose of a feasibility analysis is to:
- Determine the viability of a business idea
- Identify potential obstacles and challenges
- Assess resource requirements
- Evaluate market potential
- Minimize risk before significant investment

### Components of Feasibility Analysis

A comprehensive feasibility analysis includes four distinct components:

#### 1. Product/Service Feasibility Analysis
This component assesses the overall appeal of the product or service being proposed. It determines whether the product or service makes sense in the marketplace and has the potential to attract customers.

#### 2. Industry/Target Market Feasibility Analysis
This component focuses on the industry attractiveness and the target market for the proposed product or service. It evaluates the size, growth potential, and competitive dynamics of the target market.

#### 3. Organizational Feasibility Analysis
This component assesses whether the proposed venture has sufficient management expertise, organizational competence, and resources to successfully launch.

#### 4. Financial Feasibility Analysis
This component focuses on the financial aspects of the proposed venture, including total startup cash needed, financial performance of similar businesses, and the overall financial attractiveness of the proposed venture.

## Product/Service Feasibility Analysis

Product/service feasibility analysis determines whether a proposed product or service has the potential to attract customers. This analysis has two components:

### 1. Usability Assessment
This aspect focuses on whether the product or service is usable, functional, and appealing to potential customers. It addresses questions such as:
- Is the product easy to use?
- Does it solve a real problem?
- Is it superior to existing alternatives?
- Will customers find it attractive?

### 2. Novelty Assessment
This aspect determines whether the product or service is novel and differentiated from existing offerings. It addresses questions such as:
- Is the product or service unique?
- Does it offer something new to the market?
- Can it be easily copied by competitors?
- What makes it stand out from alternatives?

### Primary Research Techniques

Several techniques can be used to assess product/service feasibility:

#### Concept Tests
A concept test is a preliminary evaluation of a new product idea, consisting of a written description of the product and its benefits. Potential customers are asked to evaluate the concept and provide feedback.

#### Focus Groups
Focus groups bring together 6 to 10 potential customers to discuss a product idea in a guided, informal setting. The goal is to gather qualitative feedback about the product's potential appeal.

#### Prototype Tests
A prototype is an early sample, model, or release of a product built to test a concept or process. Prototype testing allows potential customers to interact with a tangible version of the product.

#### Beta Tests
Beta testing involves releasing a nearly complete version of a product to a limited audience before making it available to the general public. This provides real-world feedback before full launch.

## Industry/Target Market Feasibility Analysis

Industry/target market feasibility analysis assesses the overall appeal of the market for the proposed product or service. This analysis has two components:

### 1. Industry Attractiveness
This aspect evaluates the overall characteristics of the industry in which the proposed venture will operate. Key factors include:
- Industry size and growth rate
- Competitive intensity
- Entry barriers
- Cost structure
- Distribution channels
- Industry trends

### 2. Target Market Attractiveness
This aspect evaluates the specific segment of the market that the proposed venture will target. Key factors include:
- Market size and growth potential
- Customer needs and preferences
- Purchase behavior
- Demographics and psychographics
- Accessibility and reachability

## ORGANIZATIONAL FEASIBILITY ANALYSIS

Organizational feasibility analysis assesses whether a proposed venture has the necessary management expertise, organizational competence, and resources to successfully launch. This analysis has two primary components:

### 1. Management Prowess
Management prowess refers to the experience and expertise of the proposed management team. Key considerations include:
- Relevant industry experience
- Track record of success
- Complementary skills
- Commitment and dedication
- Leadership capabilities

### 2. Resource Sufficiency
Resource sufficiency refers to whether the proposed venture has sufficient resources to successfully launch. Key resources include:
- Human resources
- Financial resources
- Physical resources
- Technical resources
- Network resources

## Financial Feasibility Analysis

Financial feasibility analysis focuses on the financial aspects of the proposed venture. This analysis has three components:

### 1. Total Startup Capital Requirements
This component determines the total amount of money needed to launch the venture successfully. It includes:
- Equipment and inventory
- Rent and deposits
- Employee wages
- Marketing and advertising
- Insurance and permits
- Working capital

### 2. Financial Rate of Return
This component evaluates the potential financial returns of the venture. It considers:
- Revenue projections
- Expense estimates
- Profit margins
- Return on investment
- Break-even analysis

### 3. Overall Financial Attractiveness
This component assesses the overall financial viability of the venture by considering:
- Market comparables
- Risk factors
- Investment requirements
- Potential returns
- Exit opportunities
