## COPYRIGHTS

Copyrights protect original works of authorship that are fixed in a tangible medium of expression.

### What Is Protected by a Copyright?

#### Literary Works
Books, articles, computer software, and other written works.

#### Musical Works
Songs, compositions, and accompanying lyrics.

#### Dramatic Works
Plays, screenplays, and choreographic works.

#### Pictorial, Graphic, and Sculptural Works
Paintings, drawings, photographs, and sculptures.

#### Motion Pictures and Audiovisual Works
Films, videos, and multimedia works.

#### Sound Recordings
Recorded music and other audio works.

#### Architectural Works
Building designs and architectural plans.

### Exclusions from Copyright Protection

#### Ideas and Concepts
Copyright protects expression, not ideas themselves.

#### Facts
Factual information is not copyrightable.

#### Processes and Systems
Methods of operation and systems are not protected by copyright.

#### Government Works
Works created by the U.S. government are not copyrighted.

#### Common Property Information
Calendar facts, height and weight charts, and other common information.

### How to Obtain a Copyright

#### Automatic Protection
Copyright protection exists automatically when an original work is fixed in tangible form.

#### Registration Benefits
While not required, registration provides additional legal benefits.

#### Registration Process
- Filing an application with the Copyright Office
- Paying required fees
- Depositing copies of the work

#### Duration of Protection
- Life of the author plus 70 years for individual works
- 95 years from publication or 120 years from creation for works made for hire

### Copyright Infringement

Copyright infringement occurs when someone violates one of the copyright owner's exclusive rights without permission.

#### Exclusive Rights
- Reproduction right
- Distribution right
- Public performance right
- Public display right
- Derivative work right

#### Fair Use Defense
Limited use of copyrighted material without permission for purposes such as criticism, comment, news reporting, teaching, scholarship, or research.

#### Remedies for Infringement
- Injunctive relief
- Actual damages or statutory damages
- Attorney's fees in certain cases

### Copyrights and the Internet

The internet has created new challenges and opportunities for copyright protection.

#### Digital Millennium Copyright Act (DMCA)
Provides safe harbor protections for online service providers and establishes procedures for addressing copyright infringement online.

#### Online Infringement
Digital technology makes copying and distribution easier, creating new enforcement challenges.

#### Licensing Opportunities
The internet enables new licensing and distribution models.

## TRADE SECRETS

Trade secrets protect confidential business information that provides competitive advantage.

### SAVVY ENTREPRENEURIAL FIRM: Protecting Intellectual Property: Elvis's Memory, and Intellectual Property, Live On

This section explores how intellectual property rights continue to provide value even after the death of creators.

#### Posthumous Rights
- Copyrights continue after death
- Trademark rights can be maintained indefinitely
- Estate planning for IP assets
- Licensing opportunities for deceased creators

#### Value Creation
- Continued revenue generation
- Brand extensions
- Licensing to new media
- Merchandising opportunities

The example demonstrates that intellectual property can provide long-term value when properly managed.

### What Qualifies for Trade Secret Protection?

#### Information Type
- Formulas and recipes
- Manufacturing processes
- Customer lists
- Marketing strategies
- Financial information
- Software code

#### Requirements
- The information must be secret
- It must provide competitive advantage
- Reasonable efforts must be made to maintain secrecy

### Trade Secret Disputes

Common issues in trade secret disputes include:

#### Misappropriation
Acquiring trade secrets through improper means.

#### Breach of Confidentiality
Disclosing trade secrets in violation of confidentiality agreements.

#### Employee Mobility
Balancing employee rights to mobility with employer rights to trade secrets.

#### Remedies
- Injunctive relief
- Monetary damages
- Attorney's fees in some cases

### Trade Secret Protection Methods

#### Physical Security
- Locked file cabinets
- Restricted access areas
- Secure storage facilities

#### Electronic Security
- Password protection
- Encryption
- Firewall protection
- Access controls

#### Legal Protections
- Non-disclosure agreements
- Non-compete agreements
- Employment contracts
- Visitor policies

#### Administrative Measures
- Employee training
- Clear policies and procedures
- Regular audits
- Incident response plans

## CONDUCTING AN INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY AUDIT

An intellectual property audit is a systematic review of a company's intellectual property assets and rights.

### Why Conduct an Intellectual Property Audit?

#### Asset Identification
Discovering all IP assets owned by the company.

#### Value Assessment
Determining the value of IP assets.

#### Risk Assessment
Identifying potential IP risks and vulnerabilities.

#### Strategic Planning
Informing IP strategy and business planning.

#### Due Diligence
Preparing for transactions such as mergers, acquisitions, or financing.

### The Process of Conducting an Intellectual Property Audit

#### Preparation
- Define scope and objectives
- Assemble audit team
- Gather relevant documents
- Interview key personnel

#### Asset Identification
- Catalog all IP assets
- Determine ownership
- Assess protection status
- Evaluate commercial value

#### Risk Assessment
- Identify potential infringement issues
- Evaluate freedom to operate
- Assess enforcement capabilities
- Review licensing arrangements

#### Recommendations
- Develop IP strategy
- Recommend protection measures
- Identify licensing opportunities
- Address identified risks

#### Documentation
- Prepare audit report
- Create IP database
- Update policies and procedures
- Implement recommendations
